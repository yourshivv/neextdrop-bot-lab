Neextdrop Bot Lab: paper-trading results

Two paper-trading strategies built on Freqtrade, shared to get feedback. Paper money only.

Backtest research: 3 years, 124 coins (what the rules did on past data, and everything that failed)

Paper trading only. No real money is used. Simulated fills ignore slippage, latency and liquidity limits, so live results would be worse. This is research shared for feedback, not investment advice. Do not copy these trades.
How to read these numbers
Closed trades
Only finished trades are counted. Open positions are left out until they close.
Win rate
Share of trades that ended in profit. A high win rate alone means little: many small wins can be wiped out by a few big losses.
Expectancy
Average result per trade. The range next to it is a 95% interval. If the range includes zero, the data cannot yet separate skill from luck.
R multiple
Profit measured in units of the risk taken. +2R means the trade made twice what it risked. Here R is derived from each trade's initial stop distance and is shown only when most trades have one.
Profit factor
Total money won divided by total money lost. Above 1 means profit, below 1 means loss.
Max drawdown
Biggest fall from a previous high point of the account. It shows how bad the worst stretch felt.
Exit reasons
Shown as the bot logs them. stop_loss, target and time_stop are the strategy's own rules. streak_guard is the desk's losing-streak guard for the 4-hour breakout bots (Breakout 4h and Turtle Momentum): after 3 stop-losses on one side within 6 hours it pauses that side for 12 hours and closes the trades on that side that were already open. force_exit is an emergency desk action: an alarm fires when Bitcoin moves 1.5% or more within 30 minutes (up or down), or when the desk takes 3 stop-losses on one side within 30 minutes. The desk's AI agent then decides whether to pause new trades on one side for 1 to 12 hours and/or close that side's open trades. It is a judgement call, not a fixed price level, it may act at most once every 6 hours, and it closes many trades at once.
Clustering
Trades that close in the same hour usually come from one market move or one action. They are shown as a group, because ten trades closing together are closer to one bet than ten.
Sample size
With few trades, luck dominates. Bots below the trade threshold are flagged as too early to judge.

Notes and known limits

  • Fills are simulated on exchange price data. Real fills would include slippage and delays.
  • These two bots were picked from a larger set tested since September. Choosing after seeing results flatters them, so read the numbers with that in mind.
  • Bot rules were changed during testing. Where a start date is shown, only trades from the current rules are counted.
  • Open positions are excluded until they close.
  • Trades cluster: one market move can open and close many similar trades at once, so the trade count overstates how much independent evidence there is.
  • Since early November the market mood has read weak almost every day (most coins below their 20-day average and Bitcoin below its own), so both bots have opened no new trades since 5 Nov 2026. That pause is the filter working as designed, not an outage.
